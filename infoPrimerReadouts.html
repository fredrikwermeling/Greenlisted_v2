Sanger or amplicon NGS

Both methods amplify the edited site and read it, and they want opposite things from the PCR. The Primers for choice sets the product size and how far the primers are kept from the cut; every number stays editable under Primer-BLAST settings.

Sanger — ICE / TIDE

One long Sanger trace is read across the cut, and the mixture of insertions and deletions is inferred from where the trace starts to disagree with an unedited control.

Product500 bp or more
Primers kept clear of the cut150 bp each side

The first 20 to 50 bases of a Sanger read are unreliable, so the sequencing primer has to sit well back from the edit or the trace is still settling when it arrives. There also has to be plenty of clean sequence after the cut, because that is what carries the indel signal.

Amplicon NGS

A short product is sequenced many times, and the indels are counted directly from the reads.

Product220–280 bp
Primers kept clear of the cut50 bp each side

The ceiling is set by the read length, not by a wish for a short product. On a 2×150 paired-end run the two reads have to overlap before they can be merged, which caps the amplicon near 280 bp; on a 2×250 run you can raise it to about 450.

Shorter is not better. A deletion that reaches a primer site destroys the amplicon, and that allele then vanishes from the data rather than being counted as edited, which biases the result toward looking unedited. So the product should be the longest the reads can still merge across, not the shortest that works.

The two Sanger tools

ICE (Inference of CRISPR Edits) and TIDE (Tracking of Indels by Decomposition) do the same job by different arithmetic. Both take one trace from the edited sample and one from an unedited control.

  • ICE — run it online (formerly Synthego ICE), and the source code if you would rather run it yourself. Asks for a product of 400–800 bp with the primers at least 150 bp from the cut.
  • TIDE — run it online, and Brinkman et al., Nucleic Acids Research 2014, which describes the method. Asks for 500–1500 bp with the cut about 200 bp into the read, and needs at least 100 bp of clean sequence before it.

The Sanger setting here satisfies both at once, which is why it is a single choice rather than one per tool.

For amplicon NGS

CRISPResso2 is the usual analysis, and its paper describes what it does with the reads. It quantifies indels in a window around the cut and ignores the outermost 15 bp of the amplicon, so the cut must sit well inside the product — which the 50 bp clearance here already ensures.
